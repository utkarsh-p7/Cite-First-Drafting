# Task 4 — RQ3: explainability-by-design — analytic assessment against Hoffman et al. (2018)

Method note: analytic assessment only, per PIVOT 2026-08-04 — no instruments administered, no participants, no new model runs. Framework: the explanation-goodness criteria of Hoffman, Mueller, Klein & Litman (2018), *Metrics for Explainable AI: Challenges and Prospects*, arXiv:1812.04608 (Explanation Goodness Checklist): understandability of how the system works, sufficiency of detail, completeness, actionability, usefulness to the user's goals, communication of the system's accuracy, and support for calibrated trust. Artefact features assessed: span citations, insufficient-evidence markers, line-start ADVISER FLAG conflict lines, the row-14 missing-information checklist, and invalid-span validation. Worked example: C001 (seeded fact-find/transcript horizon contradiction); contrast case: C021 (the batch's single invalid citation). All references are to the frozen batch outputs scored in Task 2.

## Assessment

**Understandability.** The evidence map explains each drafted statement by construction rather than by post-hoc rationalisation: every material sentence carries bracketed span identifiers naming the exact source field or utterance it rests on. In C001, the horizon bullet reads, verbatim: “**Investment horizon:** you have indicated “around 5 years” in the fact‑find [FF:C001:objectives.horizon_text] and “about 10 years” during the meeting [TR:C001:0004]”.

**Sufficiency of detail and completeness.** Detail is at sentence granularity — Task 2's sentence-strict scoring found 91.0% of scored statements carried valid support (1,036/1,139 pooled; mean per-case 0.909; mean unsupported rate 0.079) with evidence-map recall of 0.885 against the labelled fact inventory. Completeness is bounded and, importantly, self-describing: where evidence does not exist the artefact says so rather than filling the gap. In the absent-capacity cases the drafts carry explicit [INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE: capacity_for_loss] markers and a populated row-14 "Missing information we still need from you" checklist — all three seeded capacity gaps were disclosed this way (Task 2 §6.1). The same section also shows the mechanism's current ceiling: only 9 of 91 omitted key-instances were disclosed overall, because template-level omissions (client identity, ISA allowance) never reach the checklist.

**Actionability.** The ADVISER FLAG line converts a detected inconsistency into a named human decision. C001's flag reads, verbatim: “ADVISER FLAG: Conflict between stated investment horizon of “around 5 years” in the fact‑find and “about 10 years” expressed in the meeting transcript [FF:C001:objectives.horizon_text][TR:C001:0004]” — it cites both conflicting sources and resolves neither, which is precisely the ethical boundary: the system surfaces, the adviser judges. The baseline draft for the same case silently adopted the 5-year figure; nothing in it tells the adviser a judgement was made at all.

**Communicating accuracy and calibrating trust.** C021 supplies the contrast case. There, one citation — TR:C021:0005‑0006, a span-range format that does not exist in the corpus grammar — was mechanically checked against the assembled context and marked span_valid: false (the batch's only invalid citation, 1/1,689). The validator turns a fabricated-looking reference into a visible, localised warning instead of a latent error, giving the reviewer a per-claim signal of when *not* to trust the draft. Hoffman et al. treat this calibration — knowing when to rely on the system and when not to — as the end-goal of explanation, and it is here a property of the pipeline, not of user interpretation.

**Honest limitation.** This assessment is analytic: it establishes that the artefact's explanations have the *properties* Hoffman et al.'s criteria describe, not that practising advisers experience them as satisfying, useful or trust-calibrating. Perceived explanation quality is an empirical claim requiring practitioner evaluation (e.g. the Explanation Satisfaction Scale administered as published); that validation was removed from scope with the 2026-08-04 pivot and is stated plainly as future work.
