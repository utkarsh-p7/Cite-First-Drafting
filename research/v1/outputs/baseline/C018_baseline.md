## The advice we are giving you  

We recommend that you invest the £22,500 you have available as a lump‑sum into the Aldercroft Balanced Growth Fund.  To give you tax‑efficient growth you can place part of the investment inside a Stocks & Shares ISA and the remainder in an unwrapped General Investment Account (GIA).  Both wrappers hold the same fund and have the same risk profile, so the overall investment outcome will be the same; the ISA portion will shelter income and capital gains from UK tax, while the GIA portion will be fully taxable.

## Why this recommendation is suitable for you  

* **Match to objective and horizon** – The Aldercroft Balanced Growth Fund is designed for capital growth over a period of five years or more, which aligns with your five‑year university‑fund goal.  
* **Risk profile** – The fund carries a Summary Risk Indicator of 4 / 7, the same level as your stated risk tolerance.  It is a diversified multi‑asset portfolio (approximately 60 % global equities, 40 % bonds), providing the level of risk you are comfortable with.  
* **Capacity for loss** – Your high capacity for loss, demonstrated by a large cash buffer and strong disposable income, means you can bear the market risk inherent in the fund.  
* **Tax efficiency** – Using a Stocks & Shares ISA for part of the investment shelters any income and capital gains from UK tax, enhancing the net return for a goal that is five years away.  The remaining amount can be held in a GIA, giving you flexibility to invest any amount beyond the ISA allowance.  
* **Liquidity** – Both wrappers allow daily dealing and withdrawals are normally processed within five business days, giving you access to the money if your circumstances change.

## Risks and possible disadvantages  

* **Market risk** – The fund’s value can fall below the amount invested, especially in a bad market year.  This is a key risk of any equity‑biased investment.  
* **Currency risk** – A portion of the fund is invested in non‑GBP assets, exposing you to fluctuations in foreign exchange rates.  
* **No capital guarantee** – There is no guarantee that you will receive back your original capital at any point.  
* **ISA withdrawal limits** – The ISA is not a flexible ISA; any amount you withdraw cannot be replaced within the same tax year without using your annual ISA allowance.  
* **Tax on GIA** – Income and gains in the GIA are subject to UK income tax and capital gains tax, which will reduce the net return on that portion of the investment.
