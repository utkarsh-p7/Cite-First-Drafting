## The advice we are giving you  

We recommend that you invest the £31,500 you have available for your children’s university costs in a balanced multi‑asset fund (the Aldercroft Balanced Growth Fund).  To make the most of tax efficiency and flexibility, we suggest using **both** a Stocks & Shares ISA wrapper and an unwrapped General Investment Account (GIA) wrapper.  The investment will be made on a regular monthly basis.

## Your objectives, circumstances, knowledge and experience  

- **Primary objective:** Save £51,000 for your two children’s university expenses.  
- **Time‑frame:** Around 3 years.  
- **Age and employment:** 28 years old, employed with a gross annual salary of £168,300.  
- **Financial situation:**  
  - Cash savings: £50,000  
  - Existing investments: £52,500  
  - Liabilities: £18,000  
  - Monthly essential outgoings: £850  
  - Monthly discretionary outgoings: £1,250  
  - Emergency‑fund coverage: 58.8 months (well above the usual 3‑month recommendation)  
  - Capacity for loss: High, supported by substantial liquid assets and high disposable income.  
- **Dependants:** 2 children.  
- **Tax band:** Additional rate taxpayer.  
- **Knowledge & experience:** No previous investment products held; you trade “sometimes” and have limited experience with investment funds.  

## Why this recommendation is suitable for you  

- **Risk & return profile:** Your attitude to risk is “balanced” (ATR score 3) and you are comfortable with some ups and downs for better growth. The Aldercroft Balanced Growth Fund carries a risk indicator of 4 / 7, matching a balanced approach.  
- **Investment horizon:** Although the fund’s policy is for a 5‑year minimum holding period, the 3‑year target can still be pursued, accepting that market fluctuations may affect the final amount. Your high capacity for loss means short‑term volatility is unlikely to impact your day‑to‑day finances.  
- **Tax efficiency:** Using a Stocks & Shares ISA shelters income and capital gains from UK tax, which is valuable given your additional‑rate tax position. The GIA provides flexibility for any amount that exceeds the ISA allowance or for withdrawals that you may need to replace within the same tax year.  
- **Cost‑effectiveness:** Both wrappers have the same platform fee (0.25 % p.a.) and fund ongoing charge (0.60 % p.a.), giving an illustrative total charge of 0.85 % p.a., which is reasonable for a balanced multi‑asset fund.  
- **Liquidity:** Daily dealing and withdrawals within five business days suit your need for access, while the ISA’s non‑flexible rule is mitigated by the parallel GIA wrapper.  

## Risks and possible disadvantages  

- **Market risk:** The fund’s value can fall below the amount invested, especially over a shorter 3‑year horizon.  
- **Currency risk:** Non‑GBP holdings expose the investment to exchange‑rate movements.  
- **No capital guarantee:** There is no guarantee that you will receive back the £31,500.  
- **ISA withdrawal limitation:** Money withdrawn from the ISA cannot be replaced in the same tax year without using your annual ISA allowance.  
- **Recommended holding period:** The fund is designed for a minimum of 5 years; investing for only 3 years may increase the chance of not achieving the target amount.
